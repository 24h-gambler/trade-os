# Go-to-Market

**Beachhead** 1688 K-beauty/accessories sellers in Korea, importing from Guangzhou/Yiwu. Korea → China is the first corridor; the architecture is corridor-config driven.

**Wedge** Landed-cost checkout + 30-day credit.

---

## 1. Why this beachhead

- 1688 blocks Korean cards; every seller already uses a payment agency.
- K-beauty/accessories has high SKU turnover, frequent small orders, and thin margins — perfect for landed-cost + credit.
- The supplier cluster is concentrated (Guangzhou packaging, Yiwu accessories), so supply-first onboarding is feasible.
- Sellers are online-native (Naver Cafe, KakaoTalk, Instagram), so acquisition is community-led, not enterprise sales.

---

## 2. Supply-first strategy

**Sign suppliers before sellers.**

1. Pick one cluster (e.g., Guangzhou cosmetics packaging).
2. Offer suppliers a free **receivables account**:
   - Upload invoice, confirm Korean buyer, get paid in 24h.
   - Early payment at 1–2%/month.
   - Performance score that helps them win more Korean buyers.
3. Sign 30–50 suppliers; 10 active in the pilot.
4. Then acquire the Korean sellers who already buy from those suppliers: "Your factory already uses us."

**Why it works:** the supplier network solves cold-start and trust. The seller does not have to change factories; they just change how they pay.

## 4. Sales script

> "Show me your last five 1688 payments. I'll show you your true landed cost and your 30-day credit line in 10 minutes. If we can't beat your current all-in cost, you don't pay."

**Discovery questions**
1. Who do you buy from, and how often?
2. How do you pay today — agency, bank, or broker?
3. What did the last shipment actually cost, including duty/VAT?
4. How long from payment to Coupang payout?
5. What would you do with 30 extra days of cash?

**Objection handling**
- "I already use an agency." → "Keep the agency for logistics; use us for landed cost, documents, and credit. We'll show the delta."
- "XTransfer is cheaper." → "It is, for pure payment. We don't compete there. We handle customs, tax, and credit — the parts XTransfer doesn't."
- "Is this legal?" → "We operate as a trade agency/importer of record, with a bank partner and licensed offshore settlement. Here is the legal opinion."

## 5. Pilot design

| Element | Target |
|---|---|
| Sellers | 10 |
| Payments | 30 |
| GMV | $500k |
| Suppliers | 10 active |
| Take rate | 2.5–3.0% (concierge) |
| Losses | 0 |
| Repeat | 3 sellers reorder within 60 days |
| Time-to-pay | <1 day after documents |

**Concierge operations**
- Manual quote in a spreadsheet.
- Manual payment via bank/HK MSO.
- Manual customs broker coordination.
- Weekly reconciliation.
- Daily WhatsApp/KakaoTalk support.

**What we measure**
- All-in cost vs incumbent.
- Time from PI to factory payment.
- Document error rate.
- Seller repeat rate.
- Supplier acceptance.

---

## 8. CAC and LTV

- **CAC:** $1,500–2,500 per seller (community + referral + outbound).
- **Monthly contribution:** $450–550 per seller at $32k GMV.
- **Payback:** 3–5 months.
- **LTV:** 24–36 months × contribution = $11k–20k, before credit expansion.
- **Credit expansion:** adds $144+/month per seller; improves LTV 30–50%.

---

## 9. What not to do

- Do not run paid ads before the landed-cost calculator converts organically.
- Do not sign sellers without a supplier who can be paid.
- Do not promise "4% → 1.5%" — the comparison is the 6–11% bundle, not the 0.4% rail.
- Do not expand to a second corridor until the first has 50 sellers and 0 losses.
- Do not build a marketplace; settle existing relationships.
